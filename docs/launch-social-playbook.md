# Social-First Launch Playbook

## Positioning

CareerGem helps early-career STEM candidates turn résumé uncertainty into a role-specific 90-day plan.
The first audience is individual students, new graduates, and early-career professionals—not employers.

## Content pillars

1. **Hiring signal:** explain what a recruiter can and cannot infer from a résumé in a 10-second screen.
2. **STEM proof:** show how projects, coursework, labs, open source, and internships become credible evidence.
3. **Role targeting:** compare what changes between junior software, data/AI, cybersecurity, and engineering applications.
4. **Build in public:** share what you are learning while developing CareerGem, without presenting unverified performance claims.

## Short-form format

Use a simple 20–45 second structure: name one concrete career problem, demonstrate it with a fictional or
fully consented example, give one actionable fix, then invite viewers to try CareerGem's free assessment.

Avoid showing real résumés, user data, job-application outcomes, or client testimonials without explicit
permission. Do not promise interviews, jobs, score improvements, salary outcomes, or AI accuracy that has
not been validated.

## Weekly starter cadence

- Publish three short videos: one hiring-signal tip, one role-specific example, and one build-in-public update.
- Repost native edits to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and TikTok.
- Track views, profile visits, landing-page visits, sign-ups, first completed assessments, and paid conversions.
- Review the top-performing hook weekly; create two variations rather than changing the whole message at once.

## First experiments

- "Why your STEM project is not helping your résumé yet."
- "What a junior cybersecurity résumé must prove in ten seconds."
- "I built a tool that turns a target job into a 90-day career plan."
- "Three ways new grads accidentally sound senior without showing evidence."

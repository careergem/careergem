# First 10 CareerGem Short-Form Video Scripts

Use these as 20–35 second vertical videos for YouTube Shorts, Reels, and
TikTok. Film with a phone plus simple screen recordings of synthetic CareerGem
reports. Do not show a real user's résumé, report, name, school, or score
without written permission.

Every script ends with the same truthful call to action:

> “CareerGem is in free public beta. Run one private assessment from the link
> in my bio.”

## 1. Your résumé may be too vague to be rejected fairly

**Hook on screen:** “New grads: ‘worked on a team project’ tells hiring managers nothing.”

**Say:** “If your strongest résumé bullet is ‘worked on a team project,’ the
reader cannot tell what you built, what you owned, or what changed because you
were there. Start with the system, name your contribution, and show an outcome.
‘Built a Python data-cleaning pipeline that reduced manual review time by 40%.’
That is evidence, not an adjective.”

**Visual:** Split screen of a synthetic weak bullet, then the stronger rewrite.

## 2. Stop buying certificates before you identify the gap

**Hook on screen:** “Before you buy another certificate, find out what the job actually needs.”

**Say:** “A certificate can be useful. But if your target data role is missing
SQL evidence and your portfolio has no business question, another general course
is not the highest-impact move. Look at the role, find one missing signal, and
build proof for that signal. Your next move should be specific enough to start
this week.”

**Visual:** Synthetic report showing one ranked gap and one 30-day action.

## 3. A skills list is not proof

**Hook on screen:** “Listing Python is not the same as proving Python.”

**Say:** “Hiring teams see skills lists all day. What gets remembered is the
evidence right beside it: what you built, what constraint you solved, and what
changed. Replace a wall of tools with one project bullet that lets the reader
infer the skill. Evidence makes your résumé easier to trust.”

**Visual:** Highlight a long synthetic skills list, then a concise evidence-led project bullet.

## 4. Your first target role should be narrower

**Hook on screen:** “Applying to five job titles at once can make every application weaker.”

**Say:** “Early in your career, broad curiosity is normal. But your résumé has
to answer one question at a time: why are you a credible candidate for this
role? Pick one target role for the next 30 days. Study its signals, create one
piece of evidence, and tailor your language. You can expand later from a
stronger base.”

**Visual:** Three broad job titles collapse into one focused target and a short action list.

## 5. Internships are evidence—not errands

**Hook on screen:** “Your internship may sound smaller than it was.”

**Say:** “Do not write ‘assisted the engineering team.’ Say what you owned,
what tool or process you touched, and why it mattered. Maybe you created a test
fixture, documented a failure mode, or automated a report. The point is not to
inflate it. The point is to make the actual engineering visible.”

**Visual:** Synthetic internship bullet rewrite with coloured ownership, tool, outcome labels.

## 6. A career score should never be the conclusion

**Hook on screen:** “A career score is only useful if it tells you what to do next.”

**Say:** “A number without an action just creates anxiety. The useful question
is: what is the one gap most likely to change my next application? CareerGem
uses the score to rank gaps and point to a first move. Treat it as a direction,
not a verdict on your potential.”

**Visual:** Synthetic score transitions to the “Start here” card.

## 7. Recruiters scan for ownership

**Hook on screen:** “One word can make a project sound passive: ‘helped.’”

**Say:** “Sometimes you did help—and honesty matters. But say what your part
actually was. Did you design the test plan? Ship an API route? Validate the
dataset? ‘Helped’ hides ownership. A precise verb lets someone understand where
you can contribute on their team.”

**Visual:** Highlight “helped,” then show three accurate ownership verbs.

## 8. You do not need to publish your résumé to get feedback

**Hook on screen:** “Career help should not require posting your résumé publicly.”

**Say:** “CareerGem is built around a different idea: your résumé and report
stay encrypted in your browser. You can run a private assessment, then choose a
small text-only roadmap summary if you want to share it. Your school and
interests are optional and not used in the assessment.”

**Visual:** Screen recording of privacy page, then the Share safely area—using synthetic data.

## 9. Build in public: why CareerGem exists

**Hook on screen:** “I got tired of career advice that says ‘network more.’”

**Say:** “Most early-career candidates do not need another generic checklist.
They need to know which gap matters first for the role they actually want. I am
building CareerGem to turn a résumé and target role into a private next move and
90-day plan. It is in public beta, and the feedback is shaping what I build.”

**Visual:** Founder speaking to camera, cut to synthetic report and beta-feedback card.

## 10. The 30-day challenge

**Hook on screen:** “If you are getting rejected, do this before sending 50 more applications.”

**Say:** “Pick one target role. Run a private assessment. Choose the first
action in your roadmap. Spend 30 days building the evidence for it, then
reassess. The goal is not to become perfect. It is to make the next version of
your candidacy more credible than the last one.”

**Visual:** A simple 30-day calendar overlay, then a synthetic before/after action marker.

## Posting notes

- Put the hook in the first spoken sentence and as large on-screen text.
- Add captions manually or with the platform editor; correct technical terms.
- Use one CTA only: the beta link in bio.
- Reply to substantive comments with a video rather than a generic thank-you.
- Keep a record of each post's date, platform, hook, views, retention, profile
  visits, link clicks, signups, and first assessments.
